EEG across India

Since 2017, 1,887 people have come to the Centre for Brain Mapping at NIMHANS, Bengaluru, to have their brain's electrical activity recorded for a long-term study of five brain and mental health conditions. Patients, their families and healthy volunteers all take part. Many of them return every couple of years.

    How a recording is made

    What happens on the day

    Having an EEG doesn't hurt and involves no needles or radiation. This is what happens when someone comes to the Centre for Brain Mapping.

    Step 1

    Agreeing to take part

    A member of the study team explains what the recording involves and answers questions. Taking part is voluntary, and the person, or a family member on their behalf, gives written consent.

    Step 2

    A clean scalp

    The sensors need to touch clean skin. Hair dye, henna, oil, dandruff or lice get between the sensor and the scalp, so on those days we reschedule the recording.

    Step 3

    Measuring the head

    We measure around the head with a tape to choose the right size of sensor net. The net needs to fit snugly so every sensor sits in its proper place.

    Step 4

    Putting on the net

    The net holds 32 or 128 small sponges soaked in salt water. It goes on in a few minutes, lined up with the dip between the eyes and the bump at the back of the head. See the net in 3D →

    Step 5

    Checking every sensor

    Before recording, the computer checks how well each sensor touches the skin. Any that are not touching properly are adjusted.

    Step 6

    Recording

    The person sits quietly and relaxed. Blinking, chewing and tense muscles show up on the recording too, so staying still gives the clearest picture of the brain's own activity. Afterwards the net comes off and the hair is rinsed.

    Each recording is filed under a code number, not the person's name.
